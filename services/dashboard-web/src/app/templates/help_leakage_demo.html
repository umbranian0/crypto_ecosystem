What a leakage-aware protocol changes: the thesis's own real numbers

This platform's leakage-aware, purged walk-forward protocol (see the "Methodology" panel on any run's detail page, and the walk-forward/purge-gap explainer) was itself validated against a real Bitcoin return-modeling case study. The numbers below are that study's own published results -- not an illustrative or fabricated example.

The real, purged results (1h / 6h / 24h horizons)

Horizon Naive0 MAE OLS MAE (vs. Naive0) OLS DA DM (better/worse vs. Naive0)
1h 0.003627 0.003683 (+1.55%) 51.33% 0/4
6h 0.009110 0.009533 (+4.64%) 52.51% 4/18
24h 0.019720 0.020895 (+5.96%) 50.80% 14/22

OLS was the best non-naive challenger of the models tested (OLS, Random Forest, ARIMA, LSTM), and it still lost to Naive0 on average MAE at every horizon -- by 1.55% at 1h, 4.64% at 6h, and 5.96% at 24h. Diebold-Mariano test evidence was mixed and limited: even OLS had more "worse" than "better" splits against Naive0 at nearly every horizon.

Why a leaky evaluation would have looked better

There is no second, independently-computed "leaky" number to show here -- no leaky re-run of this study exists or is computed by this platform, and none is fabricated for this page. What follows is descriptive: a naive random-split or globally-fit-preprocessing evaluation would not purge the boundary between train and test data the way this platform's protocol does. Without a purge gap, information from rows right at the train/test boundary -- for example a rolling-average feature computed across both sides of that boundary -- can leak into the test window, giving a model an unfairly easy look at data it is supposed to be evaluated against. Fitting preprocessing (scaling, imputation, feature selection) globally rather than per training fold has the same effect at a larger scale. Either mistake tends to make a model look more competitive with, or even better than, a naive baseline than it actually is. The numbers in the table above are what this platform's own leakage-aware protocol actually reported for the same models -- the purge gap and train-fold-only preprocessing are exactly the mechanism that protects against the inflated, misleading numbers a leaky evaluation would otherwise produce.

The core finding, restated plainly

Under this leakage-free, purged walk-forward protocol, no model beat Naive0 in a stable, statistically significant way at any of the 1h/6h/24h horizons -- OLS, Random Forest, ARIMA, and LSTM all did not beat Naive0 stably. The best directional accuracy observed across every model and horizon was 52.51% (OLS, 6h) -- barely above the 50% chance level. This is exactly why the platform validates models against a naive baseline under a leakage-aware protocol by default, rather than reporting a model's own numbers in isolation.
